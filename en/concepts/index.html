Types of gestures

  • Material gesture - Transforms objects, bodies, the milieu: moving, assembling, installing.
  • Semiosic gesture - Produces codified forms: speaking, writing, diagramming, signing. It is not immaterial.
  • Gesture of reception - Gesture through which an artifact is perceived and experienced (rereading a document, for instance).

Mediating artifacts

  • Artifact autonomy - Capacité de l'artefact à circuler indépendamment de celui qui l'a formé.
  • Virtualizing artifact - Seals the commitment: a quotation, a specification.
  • Enabling artifact - Equips competence: a tool, a method.
  • Value-bearing artifact - The work, which circulates from the performer to the beneficiary.
  • Evaluative artifact - Supports the review: a grid, an invoice, feedback.

  • Artifact's mode of existence - Groups together the persistence and materiality gradients, which make the direct link with gestures.

Gradient poles

  • Value-bearing - Pole of the finality gradient: value comes to be deposited in the artifact; the work is the value-bearing artifact.

Forms of conversation

  • Ordinary conversation - Reproduction of practices: established practices are carried on.
  • Ritual conversation - Replenishment: the participants and the collective self are reaffirmed.
  • Inventive conversation - Reinvention: the work, the selves and the milieu are reinvented. An echo of Simondon.
  • Effervescent inventive conversation - Envisaged subtype: hot, punctual creation.
  • Elaborative inventive conversation - Envisaged subtype: sustained, worked-out creation.

Facets of signification

  • Praxeological semiosis - How is the transaction conducted? Collective arrangement of contributions: conversation, cooperation, coordination,…
  • Narrative semiosis - Where does the gesture stand in the trajectory? Commitment, competence, performance, review.
  • Cognitive semiosis - How is the gesture or the artifact understood? Sensible, practical, conceptual.
  • Performative semiosis - What does the transaction produce, what does it transform? Creation, subjectivation, territorialization.

Performative dimensions

  • Creation - Resource mediating artifacts become a work.
  • Subjectivation - Living actants become transformed selves.
  • Territorialization - The environment becomes an appropriated milieu.

Cognitive reasons

  • Sensible reason - Situated or qualitative sense: signal, singular, experienced here and now.
  • Practical reason - Typified or referential signification: type, genre, what one knows how to do with it.
  • Conceptual reason - Theoretical or hypothetical signification: concept, category; includes narratives and ideologies.

Moments of the narrative trajectory

  • Consummatory - The two faces of performance: accomplishment on the performer's side; reception, use and appropriation on the…
  • Narrative trajectory - Also called transactional trajectory: a sequence oriented toward the work, from the problematic situation to the…
  • Commitment - Project owner and entrepreneur commit themselves: demand and offer.
  • Competence - Belongs to the entrepreneur: confers an ability to do.
  • Performance - Two faces: realization and reception.
  • Realization (accomplishment) - Face of the performance on the performer's side: “done”.
  • Reception (use and appropriation) - Face of the performance on the beneficiary's side: making the work one's own, integrating it, diverting or extending it.
  • Review - Closes the trajectory and can restart another; includes evaluation and retribution.
  • Evaluation - Judgment of the result; accomplishing is not evaluating.
  • Retribution - Payment, thanks, recognition.

Phases of the act (Mead)

  • Impulse - First phase of the act in Mead.
  • Perception - Second phase of the act in Mead.
  • Manipulation - Third phase of the act in Mead.
  • Consummation - Fourth phase of the act in Mead.

Transactional roles

  • Project owner / beneficiary self - Pole of the reception of the work: demand, reception, use and appropriation, evaluation, retribution.
  • Entrepreneur / performer self - Pole of the realization of the work: vision, commitment through the offer, competence, accomplishment.
  • Project owner - Expresses a demand; takes part in the commitment and the review.
  • Entrepreneur - Makes an offer; takes part in the commitment and competence.
  • Performer - Accomplishes the work during the performance.
  • Beneficiary - Receives and appropriates the work; takes part in the performance and the review.

Regimes of cooperation

  • Contributive regime - Converging: contributing to a common work and recognizing one another.
  • Competitive regime - Rivaling: aiming at the same scarce value according to shared rules.
  • Antagonistic regime - Opposing one another while continuing to interact through common artifacts.
